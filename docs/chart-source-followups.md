# Measurement follow-ups

These are source requirements for potential additions, separate from the observed chart series. Existing observations and the default observed-only views remain authoritative.

## Recording hours and clinical coverage

- The existing **Implanted BCI participants over time** chart already provides dated, separately reported cohorts. The cohorts overlap; a field-wide installed-base total cannot be obtained by adding them.
- An aggregate BrainGate recording-corpus total is unverified. Card et al., [*Long-term independent use of an intracortical brain–computer interface for speech and cursor control*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04414-6), published June 15, 2026, reports over 3,800 hours of one participant’s independent system use. That is a useful device-use measurement. Before adding it to a recording-data chart, establish how system-on time maps to retained neural recordings, distinguish task and freeform use, and retain the paper’s access restrictions. It does not establish the aggregate recording time of BrainGate participants.
- A clinical-benefit view needs an explicit endpoint: demonstrated communication, computer access, movement, or another measured function; eligible participant count; study design; follow-up; and a source for each claim. Study indications, restored functions, regulatory clearances, and cured diseases need separate fields. A clinical-benefit counter is deferred until its numerator and denominator are defined and audited.

## Reconstruction and proofreading cost

The existing error trade-off and proofreading-throughput evidence measures different parts of reconstruction. A dollar-cost chart additionally needs realized expenditure or labor rates, processed volume, voxel geometry, tissue/species, reconstruction target, and an independently specified quality endpoint. Project-wide person-years and illustrative per-neuron effort do not establish a comparable dollar cost per unit volume. Acquisition-budget scenarios exclude reconstruction and proofreading.

## Public and philanthropic capital

A broader field-capital view needs a separate selected-program/award ledger, with payer, recipient, jurisdiction, native currency, date, capital type, and whether the amount is a budget, award, commitment, or disbursement. Program envelopes and their subawards must be linked to prevent double counting. Company financing histories do not establish complete field coverage.

First-party program records, such as [ARIA’s Precision Neurotechnologies programme](https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/scalable-neural-interfaces/precision-neurotechnologies), are useful discovery sources. A program listing is insufficient evidence for cash disbursed or for a complete annual field total. No such aggregate is introduced here.

## Comparability and extrapolation

Resource bytes depend on image product, resolution, encoding, pyramid levels, and included derivatives. Anatomical neuron counts provide a separate scale reference. Converting an anatomical target to bytes requires explicit acquisition and storage assumptions.

A last-12-month trend fitted across different resources would mix changes in specimen, product, and measurement basis. A projection requires comparable repeat observations of a defined series; sparse cross-resource checkpoints remain observed points. The publication-growth view likewise describes endpoint output growth; causal explanations and within-period acceleration need separate evidence.
